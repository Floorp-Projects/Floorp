### Explicit hard-supervisor-loss boundary

Locations: candidate `_execute_owned`, `_cleanup_owned`, and parent `execute` at snapshot lines 420, 381 and 478. In one own-process test, the controlled child sends SIGKILL to its birth-verified supervisor. In another it sends SIGSTOP; parent eventually terminates/kills the supervisor. Parent fails closed in both cases: ValueError at 0.114 seconds and TimeoutExpired at 14.352 seconds. The owned child survives until fixture cleanup; the supervisor is gone. Both children are then pid+birth checked, killed through pidfd, and reaped by the isolated fixture subreaper; no residual fixture process remains.

This reproduces a real containment limit. Killing only the supervisor cannot recover its lost adopted-descendant identity, and the parent has no independent job cgroup/namespace ledger. It is distinct from required normal/timeout/init/log-failure cleanup and from the normal detached-child bug under repair. Do not claim universal SIGKILL/kernel-stop containment from PID bookkeeping. Document whole-job disposable-worker teardown as the hard-failure boundary; a future shared/self-hosted execution needs an externally enforced dedicated job scope. The existing private native launcher already requires whole-cgroup teardown, but its configuration was not exercised here. This source-shadow limit does not justify cancelling the existing normal hosted PR CI.
